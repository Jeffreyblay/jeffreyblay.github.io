● Journal · Remote Sensing – MDPI
Geospatial and Deep Learning Approaches for Modeling Floodwater Depth in Urbanized Areas
ResNet18Flood Depth LiDARHydrostatic Principle
Floodwater depth estimation is essential for disaster response and infrastructure planning yet remains challenging in urban areas with limited gage and hydrological data. This study presents a deep learning-based framework grounded in the hydrostatic equilibrium principle to estimate flood depth using a remote sensing approach. ResNet18 outperformed deeper models, achieving RMSE of 0.71 ft, MAE of 0.23 ft, SSIM ≈ 99% and R² ≈ 94%.

● Journal · Elsevier – Data in Brief
Inundation2Depth: A Multi-Source Dataset for Floodwater Depth Estimation in Urban Areas
Benchmark Dataset5,925 Tiles CarolinasHurricane Matthew
Floods impose severe risks in urban areas, yet operational mapping often stops at inundation extent rather than depth. Inundation2Depth pairs inundation extent-depth labels derived from aerial imagery and LiDAR-based DTMs under hydrostatic assumptions. The dataset encompasses 12 flood-affected areas across North and South Carolina covering 24,649.88 acres with 5,925 overlapping tiles.

● Journal · IEEE Access
Multi-Resolution Data Fusion for Resilient Flood Mapping
SAR FusionU-Net UAVSARSentinel-1
This study presents a comparative analysis of flood mapping using multimodal sensor fusion and semantic segmentation. Integrating high-resolution UAV RGB imagery with SAR data from Sentinel-1 and UAVSAR, a modified U-Net architecture handles multimodal stacked inputs. The combination of RGB UAV with UAVSAR VV polarization at 5 m resolution achieves the highest performance (mean IoU: 84.9%), outperforming RGB UAV alone (77.8%).

Real-Time Traffic Insights with Physics-Informed Neural Networks: Integrating the Aw-Rascle Model and LLMs
Physics-Informed NNTraffic Flow LLMAw-Rascle Model
This paper introduces PINN-TSE, a Physics-Informed Neural Network-Based Traffic State Estimator integrating the Aw-Rascle traffic flow model with machine learning and NLP. Validated on real-world US-101 highway data, PINN-TSE achieved MAE of 2.4 vpm for density and 3.98 mph for velocity — improvements of 60% and 73% respectively over purely data-driven models.

Dark Development: Satellite Analysis of Building Density and Electricity Provision in Ghana's Urban Areas
Urban AnalyticsNighttime Light GhanaU-Net Regression
This study explores the spatial distribution of building density and its co-occurrence with electricity infrastructure in metropolitan cities across Ghana. Using Landsat and nighttime light satellite imagery with a UNet regression algorithm, 21% of Kumasi's urban area, 9.6% in Tamale, 0.9% in Accra, and 0.5% in Sekondi-Takoradi exhibit high building density but limited electricity provision.

DeepFlood for Inundated Vegetation: High-Resolution Dataset for Accurate Flood Mapping and Segmentation
DeepFloodSAR Inundated VegetationCNN
DeepFlood is introduced to address the requirement for high-quality training datasets. This novel dataset comprises high-resolution manned and unmanned aerial imagery and Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery, enriched with detailed labels including inundated vegetation — one of the most challenging areas for flood mapping. DeepFlood enables multi-modal flood mapping and mitigates limitations in existing datasets.

Pixels to Insights: Deep Learning for Floodwater Depth Mapping in Settlement Areas
U-NetResNet-18 Hurricane MatthewRMSE 0.11
This study maps maximum floodwater depth in Kinston, North Carolina using high-resolution aerial imagery and LiDAR for Hurricane Matthew. A UNET regression model with ResNet-18 backbone was employed to predict floodwater depth by integrating floodwater extent boundaries and terrain data based on physical principles. The model achieved RMSE ≈ 0.11, predicting max depths of 4.8 ft and 4.7 ft for two test areas.

Advanced Geo-Data Analytics and AI for 3D Flood Mapping to Protect Built Assets
3D Flood MappingU-Net 93% Accuracy LiDARInfrastructure Risk
This study investigates the impact of Hurricane Matthew on built assets in Greenville, North Carolina using integrated floodwater extent mapping, depth estimation, and impact assessment. The pretrained U-Net model achieved 93% accuracy in mapping flood extent while depth estimates yielded RMSE of 0.75. Results revealed approximately 32% of developed land, 26% of buildings, and 66% of roads were affected.

Flood Impact Risk Mapping in Settlement Areas from a 3D Perspective: A Case Study of Hurricane Matthew
3D Flood MapUAV Imagery LiDAR DEMHurricane Matthew
This study investigates an approach to map 3D flood extent and depth using UAV high resolution imagery and LiDAR for Hurricane Matthew. A deep learning approach maps flooded areas from post-event UAV images, then spatial statistics estimate water depth leveraging the DEM. Settlement areas in Grifton exhibit different risk levels from a 3D flood depth perspective, significantly enhancing near real-time emergency response strategies.

Urban Growth and Land Surface Temperature Dynamics: Lessons from Ghana
Urban Heat IslandLST LandsatGhana
This study assessed the spatial and temporal extent of urban expansion and resultant land surface temperature (LST) changes in the Awutu Senya East Municipal, Ghana. Landsat 7 satellite images between 1991 and 2018 were classified into four categories. Results showed significant increase in Built-Up areas and a positive correlation between expansion of constructed areas and increases in LST and NDBI values.
