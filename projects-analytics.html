DA — 01
Text Data Mining of Flood Depth Research Literature with R
R wordcloud2 tidyverse tidygraph FactoMineR dplyr ggplot2 Network Analysis
Applied R text analytics to a corpus of flood depth estimation research papers — extracting dominant methods, evaluation metrics, and structural relationships between flood types and data variables. Insights directly informed model selection and literature positioning in subsequent deep learning research.
Objective
To analyze research on floodwater depth estimation to identify key methods, evaluation metrics, and relationships between flood types and data variables — synthesizing a fragmented literature systematically.
Method
Applied R text analytics libraries — wordcloud2, tidyverse, tidygraph, FactoMineR, dplyr, ggplot2 — to extract, process, and visualize text data from peer-reviewed research papers on flood depth estimation.
Outcome
Network graphs revealing flood type × data variable interactions. Word clouds of dominant depth estimation methods and evaluation metrics. Insights useful for environmental modeling and flood-risk assessment strategy.
Key Findings
  • Network analysis revealed clustering between SAR-based methods and pluvial flood types, with optical imagery more associated with fluvial events.
  • RMSE and MAE emerged as the dominant evaluation metrics across flood depth literature, followed by R² and Bias.
  • Deep learning methods (U-Net, CNN variants) showed increasing dominance over traditional hydraulic models in recent publications.
DA — 02
Multivariate Environmental Index Prediction — 38-Country Survey Analysis
R GLM Two-Way ANOVA car library ggplot2 Multivariate Analysis Policy Insights
Analyzed a global environmental survey dataset (31,000 responses across 38 countries) using General Linear Models and two-way ANOVA to identify key predictors of environmental index — generating insights applicable to policy, sustainability strategy, and environmental planning.
Objective
To identify key predictors of environmental index from 31,000 global survey responses across 38 countries, providing insights applicable to policy, sustainability, and environmental strategy.
Method
Applied R for multivariate analysis using the car library to perform GLM modeling and two-way ANOVA. Generated interaction plots and residual diagnostic plots for model assessment and validation.
Outcome
Identified Education, Age, Gender, and Country as the most impactful predictors of environmental index — providing actionable insights for environmental planning and policy assessment across global contexts.
Key Findings
  • Education level was the strongest single predictor of environmental index, with higher education consistently correlated with more pro-environmental attitudes.
  • Country-level effects explained significant variance, confirming that national context (policy, culture, infrastructure) shapes environmental attitudes beyond individual factors.
  • Significant interaction effects between Age and Gender were detected, suggesting generational and gender dynamics vary meaningfully across regions.
  • Model residuals were well-behaved, validating GLM fit for this multivariate survey dataset.
DA — 03
Movie Genre ROI Analytics
R lattice plotrix car One-Way ANOVA ROI Analysis Statistical Inference
Analyzed the profitability of the six most prevalent movie genres by comparing percentage returns (revenue ÷ budget × 100) — using one-way ANOVA to statistically test whether genre meaningfully predicts return on investment, and visualizing distributions to communicate investment strategy insights.
Objective
To analyze the profitability of six movie genres by comparing percentage returns (revenue/budget × 100) and uncover statistical patterns to inform production and investment decisions.
Method
Used R libraries — lattice, plotrix, car — to clean, process, and visualize the dataset. Applied one-way ANOVA to statistically compare profit returns across genres and generate confidence interval plots.
Outcome
Revealed significant ROI differences between genres (p < 0.05). Strip plots and mean/CI visualizations communicated actionable insights for strategic decision-making in film investment and production planning.
Key Findings
  • One-way ANOVA confirmed statistically significant differences in ROI across the six genre groups (p < 0.05).
  • Horror and thriller genres showed the highest median ROI relative to production budget — low-budget films in these categories yielded disproportionately high returns.
  • Action and sci-fi genres showed high variance in returns — high-budget productions are either very profitable or significant losses, indicating higher investment risk.
  • Drama showed the most consistent, mid-range ROI — a lower-risk genre for investors seeking predictable returns.
